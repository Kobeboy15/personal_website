Anyone else seeing this pattern on LinkedIn Jobs?

First I saw micro1 posting frontend/software roles directly. Then I started seeing third-party listings that looked like normal engineering jobs, but clicking through routed applicants into micro1's platform. Now I'm seeing roles from "Hired" that appear to do the same thing.

My frustration is not that micro1 can never connect people with real paid work. I have seen people say they were paid through micro1 for short projects. That is not the issue.

The issue is transparency.

If the actual model is short-term project work, contract work, or freelance-style gigs where you finish one project and then have to interview again for the next one, then it should be marketed that way.

That is very different from posting roles that look like regular frontend/software engineering jobs. A "Senior Frontend Software Engineer" role implies something very different from "join a platform and compete for temporary project work."

Job seekers should not have to discover halfway through the funnel that what looked like a job may actually function more like project-based freelance work.

Other applicants have also publicly described frustrating experiences with micro1: repeated interview invitations, AI-conducted video interviews, technical issues, no human follow-up, no feedback, and then another invite appearing later as if nothing happened. I cannot verify every story, but when enough people are describing the same opaque process, it deserves scrutiny.

LinkedIn should be asking basic questions before allowing these listings:

- Is there a real open role?
- Who is the actual employer?
- Is this full-time employment, contract work, or freelance project work?
- How long is the engagement expected to last?
- Does the applicant have to re-interview after each project?
- Is the applicant being considered for the specific role listed, or just being added to a talent platform?
- What happens to interview recordings and applicant data?

There is nothing wrong with contract work when it is presented honestly. There is nothing wrong with freelance platforms when candidates understand what they are joining.

But listing platform-based project work as if it were a normal full-time engineering role wastes applicants' time and damages trust.

Job seekers are already dealing with ghost jobs, fake urgency, automated rejections, unpaid assignments, and endless AI screening. LinkedIn should not make that worse by allowing job posts that blur the line between employment and project-based candidate acquisition.

If a posting says "Frontend Engineer," "JavaScript Developer," or "Senior Frontend Software Engineer," applicants should not have to play detective to figure out what kind of work is actually being offered.

Transparency should be the minimum standard.
